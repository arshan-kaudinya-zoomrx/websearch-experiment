## q005 · readout

**Objective:** SOR102 safety findings and adverse events in Crohn's Disease Ulcerative Colitis  
has_answer **yes** · confidence 0.78 · 7 items · luna 31611 ms

In SOR102-101 (NCT06080048), 64 participants were enrolled across three parts; 48 received SOR102 and 16 placebo. In part 1, TEAEs occurred in 3/24 SOR102 recipients (13%: two in the 135 mg arm and one in the 405 mg arm) and 2/8 placebo recipients (25%). The SOR102 events were mild diarrhea lasting about 17 hours (probably related) and moderate dysmenorrhea lasting about 2.5 days (not related) in the 135 mg cohort, and moderate dysmenorrhea lasting about 12 hours (not related) in the 405 mg cohort. Placebo events were mild headache lasting about 4 hours and moderate catheter-site pain lasting about 5 hours; both were not related. In part 2, healthy participants received 1,215 mg twice daily for 7 days; no adverse events were reported. Across parts 1 and 2, no serious adverse events, clinically significant laboratory, ECG, vital-sign or physical-examination findings, or discontinuations were reported, with no safety or tolerability concerns noted at single doses up to 3,645 mg or multiple doses of 1,215 mg twice daily. [E1][E4]  
In part 3, among patients with active ulcerative colitis treated for 42 days, at least one TEAE occurred in 7/16 SOR102 recipients (44%: 2/7 receiving 810 mg once daily and 5/9 receiving 810 mg twice daily) versus 3/6 placebo recipients (50%). Two patients in the twice-daily group had worsening ulcerative colitis leading to discontinuation; one case was severe, was the only serious TEAE reported, and was not considered treatment-related. All other TEAEs were mild to moderate. Overall, SOR102 was described as well tolerated in healthy participants and patients with ulcerative colitis, with a low incidence of TEAEs; adverse events were predominantly mild to moderate and no new safety signals were observed. [E2][E3][E4][E5]
