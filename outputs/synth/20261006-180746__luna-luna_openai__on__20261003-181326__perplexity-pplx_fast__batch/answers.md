AZD5148 is a humanized anti-TcdB monoclonal antibody that binds the glucosyltransferase domain (GTD) and blocks delivery of its enzymatic cargo into host cells. It was formerly called PA41; its Fc includes a YTE substitution (M252Y/S254T/T256E). [E2][E6] In studies, its epitope was 99.58% conserved versus 82.47% for bezlotoxumab across 9,134 global isolates, and AZD5148 neutralized eight recently circulating ribotypes in vitro. [E1][E6] AZD5148 had in-vitro EC50 values 1,000–14,000-fold lower than bezlotoxumab against TcdB1 and TcdB2; in mouse CDI models it protected against weight loss and mortality at significantly lower doses, and adding anti-TcdA mAb PA50 provided no additional protective benefit. It also protected in a gnotobiotic piglet model. [E2][E6]

The PRISM study, NCT07285213, is listed as Phase IIb by AstraZeneca and Phase 2 by ClinicalTrials.gg; the latter listing marked it recruiting and was updated August 12, 2026. The study is for prevention of CDI recurrence in adults aged 18 years and older, with 230 participants; the design is randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled, with AZD5148 or placebo, efficacy as the primary endpoint and safety and pharmacokinetics as secondary endpoints. [E4][E9] Participants are randomized 1:1 to a single AZD5148 or placebo dose, given by intramuscular injection or intravenous push at the investigator’s choice. The stated qualifying episode includes a history of at least three unformed stools in 24 hours for two consecutive calendar days and a positive local C. difficile toxin test on a stool sample collected during the episode; diarrhea need not be present on the day of IMP administration. [E9] AstraZeneca lists first-patient-consent timing as Q4 2025 and data anticipated after 2027. [E4]

AZD5148’s Phase I study, NCT06469151, enrolled 84 healthy volunteers in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-escalation design. The listed cohorts were dose 1 intramuscularly; dose 2 intramuscularly; dose 2 intramuscularly in Chinese participants; dose 2 intravenously; dose 3 intravenously; dose 3 intravenously in Chinese participants; and dose 4 intravenously. Safety was the primary endpoint and pharmacokinetic parameters were secondary endpoints; first-patient consent was listed as Q2 2024, last-patient consent as Q4 2024, and data readout as Q1 2026. [E4]

Bezlotoxumab (Zinplava; also called CDB1) is an anti-TcdB monoclonal antibody that binds the toxin’s CROPS region. It was approved in 2016 and as a single 10 mg/kg intravenous infusion in the USA and European Union for prevention of recurrent CDI in high-risk individuals receiving antibacterial treatment. It reduced recurrence, but its production was discontinued in January 2025; another source says it was withdrawn from the market by its sponsor. [E1][E2][E7][E10]

**Objective:** SOR102 safety findings and adverse events in Crohn's Disease Ulcerative Colitis  
has_answer **yes** · confidence 0.78 · 10 items · luna 39012 ms

In SOR102-101, parts 1 and 2 had no serious adverse events; no treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in part 2. In part 1, TEAEs occurred in 3/24 (13%) SOR102 recipients and 2/8 (25%) placebo recipients. Reported SOR102 events were mild diarrhoea, moderate dysmenorrhoea and mild flatulence at 135 mg, and mild dysmenorrhoea at 405 mg; only the mild diarrhoea was considered treatment-related. Placebo events were mild catheter-site pain and mild headache. No safety or tolerability concerns were noted at single doses up to 3,645 mg or multiple doses of 1,215 mg twice daily, and no clinically significant laboratory, ECG, vital-sign or physical-examination findings were observed. [E1][E4][E5]

In part 3, TEAEs occurred in 7/16 (44%) SOR102 recipients and 3/6 (50%) placebo recipients; five patients in the 810 mg twice-daily group and two in the 810 mg once-daily group had at least one TEAE. The poster gives TEAE rates of 57% for twice-daily SOR102, 29% for once-daily SOR102 and 50% for placebo; its table lists 5, 2 and 6 total TEAEs in the respective twice-daily, once-daily and placebo groups, and 13 overall. Most TEAEs were mild to moderate. One mild upper abdominal pain event was considered possibly treatment-related. Two patients in the twice-daily group had TEAEs of worsening ulcerative colitis leading to discontinuation; one was severe, serious and required hospitalization during follow-up, and was considered unrelated to SOR102. The study poster lists five discontinuations overall: two for worsening ulcerative colitis, two for study-schedule non-compliance and one for consent withdrawal. [E1][E2][E3][E7][E10]

No clinically meaningful effects on ECGs, vital signs, physical examinations or safety laboratory parameters, and no deaths, were reported. No detectable SOR102 or monomers were found in serum in parts 1 and 2; in most samples tested in part 3, they were also undetectable. ADAs were detected in 2/8 (25%) subjects in part 2 and 6/16 (38%) patients in part 3; some positivity was present at baseline, most treatment-emergent titers were low, and most titers were below 1:240 and were not associated with safety. A separate presentation notes low-level ADA-positive signals in 3 of 40 samples and in 2 subjects. [E1][E4][E7][E10]

**Missing:** SOR102 safety findings and adverse-event data in Crohn's disease; longer-term safety outcomes; a separate overall incidence rate for Grade ≥3 events beyond the described severe event; and incidence data for adverse events of special interest.
