In the phase 2 induction trial NCT05156125, safety analyses included patients who received at least one study dose. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 33/70 (47%) receiving tamuzimod 60 mg, 34/73 (47%) receiving 30 mg, and 24/70 (34%) receiving placebo; most adverse events were mild or moderate. The most frequently reported TEAEs (reported for events occurring in at least 5% of any treatment group) were upper respiratory tract infection: 6/70 (9%), 1/73 (1%), and 1/70 (1%), respectively; anaemia: 3 (3%), 4 (5%), and 6 (9%); and headache: 4/70 (6%), 5/73 (7%), and 2/70 (3%). No adverse events of atrioventricular block, bradycardia, macular oedema, severe or opportunistic infections, malignancies, or deaths occurred. [E8]  
**UEG Week 2024** For the phase 2 long-term extension through week 52, Prof. Danese said there were no major adverse events; the 30 mg and 60 mg maintenance treatments were described as well tolerated for up to 52 weeks. **Objective:** SOR102 safety findings and adverse events in Crohn's Disease Ulcerative Colitis  
has_answer **yes** · confidence 0.78 · 10 items · luna 33311 ms

In healthy participants, SOR102 was described as safe and well tolerated at single doses up to 3,645 mg and multiple doses of 1,215 mg twice daily. No serious adverse events, clinically significant laboratory, ECG, vital-sign or physical-examination findings, or discontinuations were reported; Part 2 had no adverse events. In Part 1, one treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE) was considered related: mild diarrhea after 135 mg, lasting about 17 hours and considered probably related. Other listed events were dysmenorrhea after 135 mg (moderate, about 2.5 days; not related), flatulence after 135 mg (mild, about 12 hours; not related), headache after placebo (mild, about 4 hours; not related), catheter-site pain after placebo (moderate, about 5 hours; not related), and dysmenorrhea after 405 mg (moderate, about 12 hours; not related). [E2] In Part 1, 13% of participants receiving SOR102 (2 in the 135-mg arm and 1 in the 405-mg arm) had a TEAE, compared with 25% receiving placebo; no TEAEs were reported in Part 2. [E2][E6] Part 1 involved 24 healthy participants assigned to 135, 405, 1,215 or 3,645 mg (6 per dose group) or placebo (8 participants); Part 2 involved 1,215 mg twice daily for 7 days (8 participants) or placebo (2 participants). [E6] In the ulcerative colitis (UC) part, patients received 810 mg once daily (7 patients), 810 mg twice daily (9 patients), or placebo (6 patients) for 42 days. At least one TEAE occurred in 44% of the 16 patients receiving SOR102 (5 in the twice-daily arm and 2 in the once-daily arm), versus 50% of patients receiving placebo. Two patients in the twice-daily arm had worsening UC leading to discontinuation; one case was severe, was not considered treatment-related, and was the only serious TEAE reported. All other TEAEs were mild to moderate. [E3][E6] Across the trial, 48 participants received SOR102 and 16 received placebo. [E6] The study was also characterized as well tolerated in healthy participants and patients with UC, with a low incidence of TEAEs; another summary characterized adverse events as predominantly mild to moderate and reported no new safety signals. [E4][E5] No detectable serum SOR102 or monomers were found in Parts 1 and 2; low-level anti-drug-antibody assay signals occurred in 3 of 40 samples: 2 of 20 for SOR102-IL-23 (one pre-dose and one post-dose sample from one subject) and 1 of 20 for SOR102-TNF (post-dose in a different subject).
