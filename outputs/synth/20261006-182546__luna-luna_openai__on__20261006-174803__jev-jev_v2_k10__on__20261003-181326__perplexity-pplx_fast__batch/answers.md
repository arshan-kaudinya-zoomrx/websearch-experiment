AZD5148 (formerly PA41), which AstraZeneca is developing, is an anti-TcdB monoclonal antibody that binds the toxin’s glucosyltransferase domain (GTD) and blocks delivery of its enzymatic cargo into host cells. It has a YTE Fc substitution (M252Y/S254T/T256E) intended to extend its half-life. One study calls it a human mAb, while another calls it humanized. [E1][E3][E4][E5] Its epitope was reported as 99.58% conserved across 9,134 global isolates, versus 82.47% for bezlotoxumab, and AZD5148 neutralized eight recent circulating TcdB ribotypes in vitro. In vitro EC50 values against TcdB1 and TcdB2 were 1,000- to 14,000-fold lower than bezlotoxumab’s; in mouse CDI models AZD5148 protected against weight loss and mortality at significantly lower doses. It also protected in a gnotobiotic piglet model and in mouse models using strains with different TcdB ribotypes. Adding the anti-TcdA mAb PA50 provided no additional protective benefit in a mouse model. [E1][E3][E4][E5]

The Phase IIb PRISM study (NCT07285213) is described as evaluating AZD5148 to prevent CDI recurrence in adults aged 18 years and above with a qualifying CDI episode. It lists 230 patients, AZD5148 or placebo, a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled design, efficacy as the primary endpoint, and safety and pharmacokinetics as secondary endpoints; first patient/participant dosed (FPCD) was Q4 2025, and data were anticipated after 2027. [E1][E5][E7] The Phase I study (NCT06469151) in 84 healthy volunteers is described as randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose escalation, with safety as the primary endpoint and pharmacokinetic parameters as secondary endpoints. Cohort 1 received dose 1 intramuscularly (i.m.) or placebo; cohorts 2a and 2b received dose 2 i.m. or placebo, with 2b in Chinese participants; cohort 3 received dose 2 intravenously (i.v.) or placebo; cohorts 4a and 4b received dose 3 i.v. or placebo, with 4b in Chinese participants; and cohort 5 received dose 4 i.v. or placebo. FPCD was Q2 2024, last patient/participant dosed (LPCD) was Q4 2024, and data readout was listed as Q1 2026. [E7]

Ozmosi lists three Phase I trials: D8820C00005, active, not recruiting, for Clostridium infections, with a primary completion date of 2025-12-29 and a 69% probability of success; jRCT2071240071, not yet recruiting, in healthy volunteers, with a primary completion date of 2026-01-12; and D8820C00002, active, not recruiting, in healthy volunteers, with a primary completion date of 2025-11-07 and a 69% probability of success. Across the 22 patients, the poster tabulates 13 TEAEs: 6 BID, 2 QD, and 5 placebo; 10/22 (46%) had at least one TEAE. [E1][E2][E5] Most TEAEs were mild to moderate. One mild abdominal (upper abdominal) pain event in the BID arm was considered possibly related to SOR102. Two BID patients had worsening UC leading to discontinuation; one worsening-UC event was severe and serious, required hospitalization during follow-up, and was considered unrelated to treatment. [E2][E3][E5] Overall, 17/22 completed per protocol (QD 6, BID 5, placebo 6); discontinuation reasons were worsening UC (2), schedule non-compliance (2), and consent withdrawal (1). [E7]

In Part 1, TEAEs occurred in 3/24 SOR102 participants (13%) versus 2/8 placebo participants (25%). With 135 mg SOR102, one participant had mild diarrhoea and moderate dysmenorrhoea, and another had mild flatulence; with 405 mg, one participant had dysmenorrhoea. Mild diarrhoea was the only TEAE deemed treatment-related. Placebo events were catheter-site pain and headache. [E1][E6] Sorriso’s UEG 2024 presentation gives durations of about 17 hours for diarrhoea, 2.5 days for 135 mg dysmenorrhoea, 12 hours for flatulence, 12 hours for 405 mg dysmenorrhoea, 5 hours for catheter-site pain, and 4 hours for headache; it calls the 405 mg dysmenorrhoea and catheter-site pain moderate, whereas two summaries call those events mild. The presentation reports tolerability at single doses up to 3,645 mg and multiple doses up to 1,215 mg BID. [E3][E8]

Across the study, no clinically significant effects were observed on ECG, vital signs, physical examination, or laboratory parameters, and no deaths occurred. [E1][E3][E4] Anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were detected in 2/8 Part 2 subjects and 6/16 Part 3 patients; some positivity was present at baseline and most treatment-emergent titers were low. The DDW poster says most titers were below 1:240 and were not associated with safety or clinical response; the UEG presentation reports low-level ADA signals in 3/40 samples and positivity in 2 subjects. The studies compared it with placebo and the component monotherapies; the company describes them as randomized, double-blind, dose-ranging studies. DUET-CD included low-, mid- and high-dose JNJ-4804 arms; Healio reports 693 randomized adults, 57% men, assigned to guselkumab, golimumab, those three JNJ-4804 dose levels or placebo. Dekavil (F8-IL-10; PF-06687234) is a fully human F8 antibody-fragment–IL-10 fusion protein: F8 binds fibronectin EDA, and the product is designed to enrich IL-10 delivery to inflamed tissue. Pfizer exclusively in-licensed F8IL10 from Philogen for development in inflammatory disorders.
